In May 2026, Bun merged a 960,000-line, 2,188-file rewrite from Zig to Rust in <strong>six days</strong>, generated mostly by Claude Code agents.<sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-1">1</a></sup><sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-2">2</a></sup> At merge it had a <strong>99.8% Linux x64 test-pass rate</strong> — and <strong>13,044 <em>unsafe</em> blocks</strong>.<sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-3">3</a></sup> For comparison, Astral's <em>uv</em> (a comparable systems-level Rust project) has 73.
</p> <p>
Then people noticed something else: parts of the original Bun test suite had been edited to make the Rust port green.<sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-4">4</a></sup> </p> <p>
The "rewrite it in Rust" meme has officially graduated to corporate strategy. Microsoft published a goal of porting <strong>one billion lines</strong> of C/C++ to Rust by 2030, AI-assisted.<sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-5">5</a></sup> DARPA's TRACTOR program is funding the same direction.<sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-6">6</a></sup> Microsoft's TypeScript compiler is being rewritten from TypeScript into Go for a ~10× speedup, due GA in 2026.<sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-7">7</a></sup> Discord's Go-to-Rust migration of their read-states service is now five years old and still cited in every distributed-systems hiring loop.<sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-8">8</a></sup> </p> <p>
Generating a cross-language port is now trivial. Anyone with a modern AI coding agent and a weekend can produce something that compiles. <strong>Producing a port that actually behaves like the original</strong> — same edge cases, same concurrency semantics, same failure modes, with the original test suite untouched — is the open problem.
</p> <p>
Port Mortem is a 72-hour hackathon. Pick a real public GitHub repo in language X. Rewrite it in language Y. Prove the original test suite still passes, unmodified. Survive a differential fuzz session. Show your <em>unsafe</em> count. Defend your architectural decisions to a human.
</p> <p>
We're not against AI-assisted porting. We're against the version of it that ships 13,000 <em>unsafe</em> blocks and edits the tests on the way out.
</p> <p class="pullquote">You have 72 hours.</p> </article>  </div> </section> <section id="why-now" class="section-gap"> <div class="container-narrow"> <header class="mb-12"> <div class="section-numeral">02 / Why now</div>  <h2 class="section-title">Why now</h2> <p class="section-lede">The “rewrite it in another language” phase of the industry stopped being a meme in 2025 and became corporate strategy in 2026.</p> </header>  <article class="prose"> <p>Microsoft is rewriting the TypeScript compiler in Go (~10× speedup), and has stated a goal of porting one billion lines of C/C++ to Rust by 2030, AI-assisted.<sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-5">5</a></sup><sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-7">7</a></sup> DARPA's TRACTOR program is funding C-to-Rust translation at scale.<sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-6">6</a></sup> Anthropic acquired Bun and shipped a 960,000-line Zig-to-Rust rewrite in six days using Claude Code.<sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-1">1</a></sup><sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-2">2</a></sup> Astral has rebuilt the entire Python tooling stack (ruff, uv, ty) in Rust and is replacing each tool people use daily.<sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-10">10</a></sup> Discord's five-year-old Go-to-Rust read-states migration is still the canonical reference for “GC vs predictable latency” in distributed systems interviews.<sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-8">8</a></sup> Cloudflare's Pingora replaced NGINX with Rust at 40M+ req/sec.<sup class="ref"><a href="#ref-11">11</a></sup></p><p>The technical capability is here. The cultural permission is here. What's missing is <strong>the rigor</strong>.</p><p>Bun shipped 13,044 <em>unsafe</em> blocks at merge and edited tests on the way out. Microsoft's billion-line goal assumes the AI-generated ports are correct without specifying how anyone will verify that at scale. Most “we rewrote X in Y” weekend posts ship without test parity, without differential fuzzing, without honest benchmarks.</p><p>This is the gap. Generating ports is solved. <strong>Proving they work</strong> is the open problem.</p><p>That's the hackathon. 72 hours. Real repo. Real test suite. Real differential fuzzing. Real signed Evaluation Protocols at the end. The north star: don't edit the tests, don't unsafe-everywhere, don't ship a port that compiles but doesn't actually behave like the original. How close you get to that bar is the score — not a pass/fail gate.</p> <p class="pullquote">Languages die. Code doesn&#39;t.</p> </article>  </div> </section> <section id="tracks" class="section-gap"> <div class="container"> <header class="mb-12"> <div class="section-numeral">03 / Eight tracks</div>  <h2 class="section-title">Eight tracks</h2> <p class="section-lede">Eight source-to-target pairs. </section> <section id="faq" class="section-gap"> <div class="container-narrow"> <header class="mb-12"> <div class="section-numeral">16 / FAQ</div>  <h2 class="section-title">FAQ</h2>  </header>  <hr class="rule-strong"> <div> <details class="faq-item"> <summary>What&#39;s the team size limit?</summary> <div class="faq-item__body">1–4 people. Solo welcome. We recommend 2–3 for the 72-hour window — bigger teams hit coordination overhead fast on a port project.</div> </details><details class="faq-item"> <summary>Can I use AI code generation?</summary> <div class="faq-item__body">Yes, and you should. Cursor, Aider, Copilot, Continue, local Ollama agents — anything from free OSS tools to paid SaaS is expected. We don&#39;t gatekeep AI use; we gatekeep the artifact. DECISIONS.md, the differential fuzz log, and the original test suite are the receipts. If the port can&#39;t be defended in writing, it scores accordingly.</div> </details><details class="faq-item"> <summary>Can I pick any GitHub repo?</summary> <div class="faq-item__body">Yes. Pick from our recommended pool, or bring your own public repo — no pre-approval needed. Your own repo should be at least 1,000 source lines, OSS-licensed, and not already ported to the target language. A test suite is optional but recommended. Track H (Open Pair) also wants the language pair justified in your README.</div> </details><details class="faq-item"> <summary>What are the LOC limits?</summary> <div class="faq-item__body">Each track has a suggested range on its card. If you bring your own repo, the floor is about 1,000 source lines and the ceiling is ~8,000. Bigger than that and 72 hours becomes a compile, not a proven port — Bun took 6 days with an agent fleet and merged with 13,000 unsafe blocks, and you have less.</div> </details><details class="faq-item"> <summary>Can I edit the original test suite?</summary> <div class="faq-item__body">You can — but it hurts your score. The file hashes are pinned at kickoff, so judges will see the diff. Leaving the originals untouched is the goal we aim at; edits are weighed against the rationale you document, not auto-disqualified. The lesson from the Bun merge is that silent edits poison the signal — if you have to touch them, explain why in DECISIONS.md.</div> </details><details class="faq-item"> <summary>What if the original repo has flaky tests?</summary> <div class="faq-item__body">Document the flakes in your DECISIONS.md, exclude them from your parity calculation with a written rationale, and judges will assess case-by-case. Don&#39;t silently skip them.</div> </details><details class="faq-item"> <summary>What counts as “passing the original test suite”?</summary> <div class="faq-item__body">The original repo&#39;s tests run against your port&#39;s binary/artifact via a thin adapter (we&#39;ll provide templates for each track). Pass rate = passing tests / total tests. ≥99% with no edits is the ideal that scores top marks — lower pass rates still score, just proportionally less.</div> </details><details class="faq-item"> <summary>Can I work on my port before July 31?</summary> <div class="faq-item__body">No code. Planning, candidate-repo scouting, agent prompt tuning, reading the source — all fine. Any port code committed before kickoff disqualifies the project.</div> </details><details class="faq-item"> <summary>What&#39;s the differential fuzz session?</summary> <div class="faq-item__body">A harness that runs the original and your port on the same inputs and diffs the outputs. We&#39;ll provide templates for CLI tools, libraries with HTTP APIs, and parser/serializer projects. Surviving 60+ continuous seconds with zero divergence earns the bonus.</div> </details><details class="faq-item"> <summary>Can the port be slower than the original?</summary> <div class="faq-item__body">Yes, but honest reporting is required. If your port is 2× slower, say so and explain why. Hiding the regression scores worse than disclosing it. Top placements need behavioral equivalence first; performance is a tiebreaker.</div> </details><details class="faq-item"> <summary>Do I have to use any specific tools?</summary> <div class="faq-item__body">No. Use whatever ships your port in 72 hours.</div> </details><details class="faq-item"> <summary>What if my port discovers a bug in the original?</summary> <div class="faq-item__body">File the upstream issue during the hackathon. Document it in your submission. That&#39;s the Bug Catcher bonus. The whole point of differential testing is that the rewriter often sees what the original maintainers missed.</div> </details> </div>  </div> </section> <section id="references" class="section-gap">
